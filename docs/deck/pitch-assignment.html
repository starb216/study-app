Alice — Landing Page, Calendar, Video Commercial

  • Slide 1: Landing Page
  • Slide 6 (Demo): Calendar + Tasks card
  • Slide 7: Runthrough / video commercial

Talking points

Slide 1 — Landing Page

  • Open the pitch: what StudyMint is and who it’s for.
  • Read the tagline: “Plan, focus, take notes, and share resources — all in one place.”
  • Point to the live URL so the audience knows where to find it.
  • Mention you built the landing page.

Slide 6 — Demo (Calendar + Tasks)

  • Explain why Calendar is central to a student’s workflow.
  • Mention you built the Calendar view and its layout.
  • Show how tasks tie to dates/subjects so students see what’s due at a glance.
  • Keep it to 2–3 sentences; pass to the next speaker smoothly.

Slide 7 — Runthrough / Commercial

  • Introduce the video commercial or live walkthrough.
  • Explain what the audience will see (real app, real features, real flow).
  • If playing a video, cue it and let it run; if live demo, narrate the first step then hand off.

Jennifer — Timer Aesthetics

  • Slide 6 (Demo): Themed Timer card

Talking points

Slide 6 — Demo (Themed Timer)

  • Explain the problem with boring timers: students abandon them.
  • Describe the themed timer visuals (forest, beach, jungle, bamboo, mountain, etc.).
  • Mention you designed the timer aesthetics — the look and feel, not the logic.
  • Emphasize how the visual theme makes focus time feel calmer and less like a chore.
  • End with the result: students actually stay for the full session because it’s pleasant to look at.

Yian — Leaderboard

  • Slide 6 (Demo): Leaderboard card

Talking points

Slide 6 — Demo (Leaderboard)

  • Explain why competition works for students: public ranks motivate consistency.
  • Describe the leaderboard: users earn shells or points for focus time and climb ranks.
  • Mention you built the leaderboard system.
  • Tie it to accountability — friends can see each other’s progress, so skipping study sessions is harder.
  • End with the social loop: focus → earn → rank → repeat.

Jean — Integration, Dashboard, Core Functions, Admin, Aesthetics, Streaks

  • Slide 2: The problem
  • Slide 3: The person
  • Slide 4: The market
  • Slide 5: The competition
  • Slide 6 (Demo): Notes, Shared Resources, Friends, Admin, Streaks, plus transition between demo cards
  • Slide 8: What works
  • Slide 9: The price
  • Slide 10: The ask

Talking points

Slide 2 — The Problem

  • State the one-sentence problem: students juggle too many apps.
  • List the apps: planner, calendar, timer, notes, sleep tracker.
  • Key line: “Nothing talks to each other.”

Slide 3 — The Person

  • Define the user: high school and university students.
  • What they want: one place to plan, focus, rest, and compete with friends.
  • Read the quote as if it came from a real user interview.

Slide 4 — The Market

  • Say the market is huge but fragmented.
  • Use the stat: 6+ apps switched per study session.
  • Conclusion: a single study-first workspace wins.

Slide 5 — The Competition

  • Contrast Notion/Calendar and Forest/Pomodoro.
  • Why they fall short: too generic or too narrow.
  • Why StudyMint wins: plan, focus, rest, notes, and shared resources in one place.

Slide 6 — Demo (Your Cards)

  • Introduce the demo slide as the team’s feature showcase.
  • For Notes: explain import/export, AI review sheets, flashcards, and quiz generation.
  • For Shared Resources / Courses: explain course-based material sharing (e.g., Integrated Maths 2, Precalc).
  • For Friends + Admin + Streaks: briefly mention social accountability, admin panel, and streak system as glue that keeps users coming back.
  • Transition between each teammate’s card so the demo feels like one coherent walkthrough.

Slide 8 — What Works

  • Share 3–4 concrete learnings from building and testing.
  • Public ranks drive competition.
  • Friends + leaderboard drive daily return.
  • Nature themes make focus feel calmer.
  • Notes + shared resources keep everything in one workspace.
  • User surveys and real feedback are driving last-minute improvements.

Slide 9 — The Price

  • State plainly: free for every student.
  • Mention future premium ideas only if asked (AI review sheets, analytics, class leaderboards).
  • Keep this slide under 20 seconds.

Slide 10 — The Ask

  • Three clear asks: try it, give feedback, tell a friend.
  • Read the URL one last time.
  • End with energy and thank the audience.
